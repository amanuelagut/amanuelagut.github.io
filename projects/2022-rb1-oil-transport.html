Oil Price Shocks and Agricultural Transport Costs in Colombia 2018–2022

Angie Manuela Gutierrez-Oviedo · Fundación Universitaria del Área Andina · 2022
FRED Data DCOILBRENTEU DANE Diesel SIPSA Transport OLS Regression Pass-Through Elasticity
Abstract
Resumen
This brief quantifies the transmission channel from international oil prices to Colombian agricultural transport costs during 2018–2022. Using FRED Brent crude data (DCOILBRENTEU), DANE diesel price indices, and SIPSA transport margin series, the analysis applies OLS regression with LINEST to estimate the oil-to-transport pass-through elasticity. The findings reveal a strong correlation (r = 0.78) between Brent crude and domestic diesel prices, with a diesel pass-through coefficient of 0.42 — meaning each 10% increase in diesel prices transmits to a 4.2% increase in agricultural transport costs. Over the 2018–2022 period, transport costs increased +34% in real terms, driven primarily by oil price volatility and the 2022 energy crisis.
Keywords: oil price transmission, diesel pass-through, transport costs, agricultural logistics, supply chain costs, Colombia
1.
Research Question
Pregunta de Investigación

What is the magnitude and speed of price transmission from international oil markets to Colombian agricultural transport costs?

H0 (Null Hypothesis): There is no statistically significant relationship between Brent crude oil prices and Colombian agricultural transport costs (correlation = 0, regression coefficient = 0).

H1 (Alternative Hypothesis): Brent crude oil prices have a positive and statistically significant effect on Colombian agricultural transport costs, with a pass-through elasticity between 0.3 and 0.6.

Transport costs constitute 15–25% of the final price for agricultural commodities in Colombia, making the oil-to-transport transmission channel a critical determinant of food price volatility. The 2018–2022 period — spanning the COVID-19 oil collapse and the subsequent 2022 energy price surge — provides exceptional variation for regression analysis.

3.
Methodology
Metodología

The analysis employs a three-stage transmission model implemented in Excel using Analysis ToolPak and LINEST functions.

Step 1 — Brent-to-Diesel correlation. Pearson correlation coefficient computed between monthly Brent crude (USD/bbl) and Colombian diesel price index, testing the first link in the transmission chain. Significance tested with t-statistic: t = r * sqrt(n-2) / sqrt(1-r^2).

Step 2 — Diesel-to-Transport regression. OLS regression estimated using LINEST: Transport_Cost = alpha + beta * Diesel_Index + epsilon. The coefficient beta represents the pass-through elasticity from diesel to transport costs. Full diagnostics reported: R^2, standard error, F-statistic, p-value.

Step 3 — Lag structure analysis. The diesel-transport regression is re-estimated with 1-month and 2-month lags on the diesel variable to identify the typical transmission delay. The lag specification with the highest R^2 and lowest residual autocorrelation is selected.

Step 4 — Period comparison. Mean transport costs compared across subperiods: pre-COVID (2018–2019), COVID collapse (2020), and post-COVID surge (2021–2022), with t-tests for statistical difference between means.

4.
Key Finding
Hallazgo Principal

The regression analysis confirms a statistically significant oil-transport transmission channel with rapid pass-through.

Key Finding
Hallazgo Principal
Brent-Diesel Correlation: r = 0.78 (p < 0.001), confirming that international oil price movements transmit strongly to Colombian diesel costs. The correlation is contemporaneous — no significant improvement with lags.

Diesel Pass-Through: beta = 0.42 (SE = 0.06, t = 7.0, p < 0.001). Each 10% increase in the diesel price index is associated with a 4.2% increase in the agricultural transport cost index. The model explains R^2 = 0.61 of transport cost variation.

Model Significance: F = 28.4 (df = 1, 58; p < 0.001), rejecting the null hypothesis that oil prices have no effect on transport costs.

Period Impact: Agricultural transport costs increased +34% from the 2018–2019 baseline (index = 100) to the 2022 average (index = 134), with the sharpest increase (+18%) occurring in H1 2022 following the Russia-Ukraine conflict.

The partial pass-through (beta = 0.42 < 1.0) indicates that transport operators absorb approximately 58% of diesel cost increases in the short term, likely through margin compression. However, during sustained price increases (2021–2022), cumulative pass-through approaches full transmission.

5.
What I Learned
Lo que Aprendí

Formal hypothesis testing — stating null and alternative hypotheses, computing test statistics, and comparing to critical values — introduced the discipline of distinguishing between "appears correlated" and "is statistically significantly correlated." This distinction is fundamental to all subsequent inferential work.

The LINEST function in Excel provides full regression output (coefficients, standard errors, R^2, F-statistic) in a single array formula. Understanding how to interpret each element of LINEST output prepared me for the more automated output of Python's statsmodels in 2024.

The concept of partial pass-through — that transmission coefficients are typically between 0 and 1 — is central to supply chain economics. This insight carries through to the corn transmission analysis (RB3) and the 2024 Capstone's multi-factor model.

6.
Limitation
Limitación
Method Limitation
Método Limitación
The OLS regression assumes linearity and does not test for non-stationarity in the time series. If Brent and transport costs are both I(1) processes, the regression may produce spurious results. The 2024 Python portfolio addresses this with formal ADF unit root tests and cointegration analysis. Additionally, the transport cost index is a composite measure that includes non-diesel components (labor, tolls), which may dilute the estimated pass-through elasticity.
7.
Policy Implication
Implicaciones de Política

The confirmed oil-transport transmission channel (beta = 0.42, p < 0.001) provides empirical grounding for food price stabilization policies. Colombia's diesel price subsidy program ("FEPC") partially insulates domestic prices from international oil volatility — the incomplete pass-through documented here (61% R^2 rather than near-100%) reflects this policy buffer.

For agricultural supply chain managers, the 1-month transmission lag provides a predictable window for cost forecasting. When Brent crude spikes, transport cost increases can be anticipated approximately 4–6 weeks later, enabling proactive contract renegotiation and logistics optimization.
