Colombia's Agricultural Trade Geography: Key Partners and Flow Patterns 2019–2023

Angie Manuela Gutierrez-Oviedo · Fundación Universitaria del Área Andina · 2023
UN Comtrade DANE Power BI Bubble Maps Treemaps Trade Geography
Abstract
Resumen
This brief applies Power BI's geographic mapping and hierarchical visualization tools to explore the spatial distribution and evolution of Colombia's agricultural trade partnerships between 2019 and 2023. Using UN Comtrade bilateral trade data, the analysis reveals that the United States commands 33.5% of agricultural exports while the European Union accounts for 21.0%. The geographic visualization exposes a North-South asymmetry: exports flow primarily to Northern Hemisphere markets while imports are sourced from Southern Cone countries, creating differential exposure to regional economic shocks.
Keywords: trade geography, agricultural exports, Colombia, UN Comtrade, Power BI maps, trade concentration, partner diversification
1.
Research Question
Pregunta de Investigación

How can geographic mapping in Power BI reveal the spatial distribution and evolution of Colombia's agricultural trade partnerships between 2019 and 2023?

H₀ (Null Hypothesis): Colombia's agricultural trade partners are geographically dispersed with no regional concentration pattern visible in map visualizations.

H₁ (Alternative Hypothesis): Colombia's agricultural trade exhibits geographic concentration, with export destinations concentrated in Northern Hemisphere markets and import sources concentrated in Southern Hemisphere countries.

The 2021 SQL-based trade network analysis identified structural patterns in numerical form. This brief asks whether geographic visualization can make those patterns more intuitive and reveal spatial dimensions — proximity effects, regional clustering, hemispheric asymmetries — that tabular analysis misses.

All bilateral agricultural trade relationships where Colombia is either reporter or partner are included, covering both exports and imports. The analysis separates traditional commodities (coffee, flowers, bananas) from non-traditional agricultural exports to identify diversification patterns.

3.
Methodology
Metodología

The brief uses Power BI's geographic and hierarchical visualization tools to explore trade network structure.

Step 1 — Bubble map of trade partners. A world map with bubbles sized by trade value shows the geographic distribution of Colombia's agricultural trade network, with color encoding for export (green) vs. import (red) relationships.

Step 2 — Treemap of product-partner composition. Hierarchical treemaps show the product composition of trade with each major partner, revealing whether relationships are diversified across multiple products or concentrated in a single commodity.

Step 3 — Year-over-year flow animation. Power BI's play axis feature animates the trade map over time (2019–2023), showing how the geographic distribution of trade shifted during and after the COVID-19 pandemic.

4.
Key Finding
Hallazgo Principal

The geographic visualization reveals that Colombia's agricultural trade network has a strongly asymmetric spatial structure.

Key Finding
Hallazgo Principal
United States commands 33.5% of agricultural exports (2023), primarily coffee and flowers. The European Union accounts for 21.0%, with Netherlands serving as the primary EU gateway for Colombian products.

China represents 6.5% of exports and growing, while Ecuador (5.7%) and Japan (4.6%) round out the top five destinations. The treemap analysis shows that export relationships are product-specific: coffee to US/EU, flowers to US, bananas to EU.

The North-South asymmetry is striking: export destinations are concentrated in the Northern Hemisphere (US, EU, Japan) while import sources are primarily Southern Cone countries (Argentina, Brazil) and the US. This creates differential exposure to regional economic shocks — Northern Hemisphere demand drives export revenue while Southern Hemisphere supply conditions drive import costs.

The temporal animation reveals a gradual eastward shift in export destinations, with Asian markets (China, Japan, South Korea) growing faster than traditional partners during 2019–2023, consistent with the diversification trend identified in the 2021 SQL analysis.

5.
What I Learned
Lo que Aprendí

Geographic visualization as hypothesis generation — the map format revealed spatial clustering patterns (Asian market growth, intra-Andean trade intensification) that were not obvious in the 2021 tabular analysis. Visual exploration generates questions that numerical analysis alone might miss.

Building treemaps for hierarchical data taught me to use visual hierarchy as an analytical tool — showing the product composition within each trade relationship simultaneously rather than requiring separate queries for each partner.

The animated play axis for temporal evolution introduced the concept of data storytelling through time — showing change as a narrative rather than a static before-and-after comparison.

6.
Limitation
Limitación
Tool Limitation
Limitación de Herramienta
Power BI maps use Bing Maps geocoding, which occasionally misplaces small trading partners or aggregates small island states incorrectly. Additionally, the bubble sizing algorithm can make small trade relationships invisible when dominant partners are included. The 2025 working paper uses formal gravity model estimation rather than visual exploration to quantify geographic trade patterns.
7.
Policy Implication
Implicaciones de Política

The North-South asymmetry in Colombia's trade geography creates differential vulnerability to regional shocks. Export revenue depends on Northern Hemisphere demand conditions (US consumer spending, EU import regulations), while import costs depend on Southern Hemisphere supply conditions (Argentine harvests, Brazilian logistics).

For ProColombia and the Ministry of Commerce, the visualization supports strategic diversification efforts. The growing Asian market share (now 11.1% combined for China and Japan) suggests that continued investment in Pacific Alliance and CPTPP market access could further reduce dependence on traditional US/EU markets.
