Chicago-to-Colombia: Corn Price Transmission Analysis 2018–2022

Angie Manuela Gutierrez-Oviedo · Fundación Universitaria del Área Andina · 2022
CME CBOT SIPSA-DANE Maiz Amarillo FRED USD/COP Granger Causality Price Transmission
Abstract
Resumen
This brief models the transmission of corn price shocks from the Chicago Board of Trade (CBOT) to Colombian wholesale markets over 2018–2022. Using CME corn futures data, SIPSA-DANE domestic "maiz amarillo" prices, and FRED USD/COP exchange rates, the analysis applies lag regression and Granger causality testing to estimate transmission speed, magnitude, and direction. The findings reveal a strong CBOT-Colombia correlation (r = 0.73), with a transmission elasticity of 0.68 and a 2-month lag. Granger causality tests confirm that CBOT causes Colombian prices (F = 8.92, p < 0.01) but not vice versa — establishing the directional flow from international to domestic markets.
Keywords: price transmission, corn futures, Granger causality, pass-through elasticity, CBOT, Colombia, maiz amarillo
1.
Research Question
Pregunta de Investigación

How do price shocks originating at the Chicago Board of Trade transmit to Colombian wholesale corn markets, and with what lag, magnitude, and causal direction?

H0 (Null Hypothesis): CBOT corn futures do not Granger-cause Colombian wholesale corn prices (lagged CBOT prices have no predictive power for Colombian prices after controlling for Colombian price history).

H1 (Alternative Hypothesis): CBOT corn futures Granger-cause Colombian wholesale corn prices with a transmission lag of 1–3 months and incomplete but significant pass-through elasticity (0.5 < beta < 1.0).

Colombia imports over 80% of its corn consumption, primarily yellow corn (maiz amarillo) from the United States. Understanding how CBOT price changes translate to Corabastos wholesale prices — including the delay, the dampening, and the exchange rate amplification — is essential for corn-dependent industries (poultry, pork, balanced feed) and food security policy.

The 2018–2022 window includes substantial price variation: the COVID-19 demand collapse (2020), the post-pandemic recovery surge (2021), and the Ukraine-conflict grain price spike (2022) when CBOT corn exceeded $8/bushel. This range provides excellent conditions for transmission analysis with 60 monthly observations.

3.
Methodology
Metodología

The analysis builds a multi-lag regression framework with Granger causality testing to estimate transmission dynamics.

Step 1 — Price co-movement visualization. Time-series charts overlay CBOT corn prices (converted to COP using USD/COP) with SIPSA domestic prices, visually identifying lead-lag patterns and divergence episodes.

Step 2 — Lag regression battery. Regression models estimated with Colombian price as dependent variable and CBOT price at lags 0, 1, 2, and 3 months as independent variable (plus USD/COP as control). The lag specification with highest R^2 and lowest residual autocorrelation identifies typical transmission speed.

Step 3 — Granger causality test. Two restricted regressions compared: (a) Colombian price regressed on own lags only, (b) Colombian price regressed on own lags plus CBOT lags. F-test compares residual sums of squares: F = [(RSS_restricted - RSS_unrestricted)/q] / [RSS_unrestricted/(n-k)]. Significant F rejects the null that CBOT does not Granger-cause Colombian prices.

Step 4 — Pass-through elasticity. The regression coefficient on CBOT price (in the best-fit lag specification) interpreted as pass-through elasticity: a 1% change in CBOT produces a beta% change in Colombian prices. Values below 1 indicate incomplete pass-through.

4.
Key Finding
Hallazgo Principal

The analysis confirms systematic price transmission from CBOT to Colombia with a 2-month lag and incomplete but substantial pass-through.

Key Finding
Hallazgo Principal
CBOT-Colombia Correlation: r = 0.73 (contemporaneous), increasing to r = 0.79 when CBOT is lagged 2 months — confirming that international prices lead domestic prices.

Transmission Elasticity: beta = 0.68 (SE = 0.09, t = 7.6, p < 0.001). Each 10% increase in CBOT corn prices transmits to a 6.8% increase in Colombian wholesale prices after 2 months. The incomplete pass-through (0.68 < 1.0) reflects inventory buffering and margin absorption by importers.

Granger Causality: F = 8.92 (df = 2, 54; p < 0.01). CBOT prices Granger-cause Colombian prices — lagged CBOT values significantly improve prediction of Colombian prices beyond what Colombian price history alone provides. The reverse test (Colombia → CBOT) yields F = 0.41 (p = 0.67), confirming unidirectional causation from international to domestic.

2022 Ukraine Shock: During the March–June 2022 CBOT surge (+45%), Colombian prices increased +38% with accelerated transmission (1-month lag instead of 2), suggesting that extreme shocks overcome normal buffering mechanisms.

The finding that pass-through increases during extreme events (2022: 84% vs. normal 68%) implies asymmetric risk: normal price fluctuations are partially buffered, but tail-risk events transmit almost fully. This has direct implications for hedging strategy design.

5.
What I Learned
Lo que Aprendí

Granger causality testing introduced the concept of temporal precedence in causal inference — the idea that causes precede effects, and that prediction improvement can establish directional relationships. The F-test framework for comparing nested models is foundational for all subsequent econometric work.

Multi-lag regression comparison taught systematic model selection. Rather than assuming contemporaneous effects, testing lag specifications 0–3 months identified the true transmission speed (2 months) that would have been missed with default assumptions.

The observation that extreme events change transmission dynamics — faster and more complete pass-through during the 2022 shock — introduced regime-dependent behavior. This phenomenon is formally captured by the GARCH models and structural break analysis in the 2024–2025 portfolio.

6.
Limitation
Limitación
Method Limitation
Método Limitación
The OLS framework does not formally test for cointegration between international and domestic prices — a necessary condition for valid inference on non-stationary time series. If both CBOT and Colombian corn prices are I(1) processes, the regression may produce spurious results unless a cointegrating relationship exists. The 2024 ARIMA work introduces stationarity testing (ADF), and the 2025 working paper applies formal Engle-Granger cointegration analysis to this same research question.
7.
Policy Implication
Implicaciones de Política

The confirmed CBOT → Colombia transmission channel (F = 8.92, p < 0.01) with 2-month lag provides a forecasting window for corn-dependent industries. Poultry and balanced feed producers can anticipate domestic price movements 6–8 weeks in advance by monitoring CBOT futures — enabling proactive inventory management and forward contracting.

For ICA (Instituto Colombiano Agropecuario) and the Ministry of Agriculture, the finding that transmission accelerates during extreme events suggests that strategic reserve releases should be timed to coincide with CBOT spikes, not delayed until domestic price increases materialize. The 2-month lag provides the policy response window.
