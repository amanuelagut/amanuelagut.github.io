Price Determinants of Rice Wholesale Markets in Colombia 2019–2022

Angie Manuela Gutierrez-Oviedo · Fundación Universitaria del Área Andina · 2022
SIPSA-DANE FRED USD/COP Arroz Paddy Arroz Blanco Multiple Regression Seasonal Dummies
Abstract
Resumen
This brief applies multiple regression analysis to identify the key determinants of wholesale rice prices in Colombian markets between 2019 and 2022. Using SIPSA-DANE domestic price data for both paddy (arroz paddy) and milled white rice (arroz blanco), FRED USD/COP exchange rates, and harvest seasonality indicators, the analysis decomposes rice price variance into macroeconomic and seasonal components. The findings reveal that USD/COP explains 38% of rice price variance, with a seasonal amplitude of +/-12% around harvest periods. The full model achieves R^2 = 0.52 (F = 14.2, p < 0.001), confirming that exchange rate dynamics and domestic production cycles jointly dominate rice pricing in Colombia's partially self-sufficient market.
Keywords: rice markets, price determinants, exchange rate pass-through, harvest seasonality, arroz blanco, Colombia
1.
Research Question
Pregunta de Investigación

Which factors — exchange rate, seasonality, or international rice prices — best explain wholesale rice price variation in Colombian markets?

H0 (Null Hypothesis): Neither USD/COP exchange rate nor harvest seasonality significantly explains Colombian rice price variation (all regression coefficients = 0).

H1 (Alternative Hypothesis): Both exchange rate and seasonal factors have statistically significant effects on rice prices, with exchange rate being the dominant factor given Colombia's reliance on imported agricultural inputs.

Rice is Colombia's most widely consumed grain, with approximately 70% domestic production and 30% imports. This mixed supply structure creates a complex pricing dynamic where both local harvest cycles and international market conditions influence wholesale prices. Understanding which factors dominate enables more effective price risk management for rice millers, wholesalers, and food security policymakers.

3.
Methodology
Metodología

The analysis employs stepwise multiple regression to identify which variables contribute independently to rice price variation.

Step 1 — Univariate correlations. Each potential determinant (USD/COP, harvest dummies) is correlated individually with rice prices to establish bivariate relationships before multivariate modeling.

Step 2 — Stepwise regression. Variables are added sequentially to the regression model using LINEST. Each addition is tested for incremental explanatory power (delta R^2). This identifies whether exchange rate and seasonality contribute independently or redundantly.

Step 3 — Full model with diagnostics. The final model: Rice_Price = alpha + beta1 * USD_COP + beta2 * Harvest_Major + beta3 * Harvest_Minor + epsilon. Full diagnostic output includes R^2, adjusted R^2, F-statistic, and individual t-tests for each coefficient.

Step 4 — Seasonal amplitude calculation. The seasonal effect is computed as the percentage deviation from the non-harvest baseline: Amplitude = (beta_harvest / mean_price) * 100. This measures the typical harvest-induced price swing.

4.
Key Finding
Hallazgo Principal

The multiple regression confirms that exchange rate is the dominant price determinant, with seasonality providing a significant but smaller contribution.

Key Finding
Hallazgo Principal
Exchange Rate Effect: beta1 = 0.82 (SE = 0.15, t = 5.5, p < 0.001). Each 100 COP/USD increase in the exchange rate is associated with a COP 82/kg increase in wholesale rice prices. USD/COP alone explains 38% of rice price variance.

Seasonal Amplitude: +/-12% around baseline. Prices drop approximately 12% during "cosecha mayor" (August–September) and rise by a similar magnitude during the inter-harvest period (November–February). The seasonal dummies add 14 percentage points to explanatory power.

Full Model: R^2 = 0.52, F = 14.2 (df = 3, 44; p < 0.001). The combination of exchange rate and two harvest dummies explains over half of rice price variation — a strong result for agricultural commodity modeling.

Paddy vs. White Rice: The exchange rate effect is stronger for milled white rice (beta = 0.92) than for paddy (beta = 0.71), reflecting the import-intensive nature of rice milling inputs (energy, machinery parts).

5.
What I Learned
Lo que Aprendí

Stepwise regression taught me to think about model building as an incremental process — testing each variable's marginal contribution rather than dumping everything into one model. The discipline of checking delta R^2 at each step prevents overfitting and clarifies which variables truly add explanatory power.

Constructing seasonal dummy variables in Excel (binary 0/1 columns for harvest months) introduced the concept of categorical encoding in regression. This technique — so simple in concept but so powerful in application — becomes standard practice in Python's pandas get_dummies() and statsmodels for the 2024 portfolio.

The product-specific finding — that rice has fundamentally different price determinant structure than corn (RB3) — reinforced the lesson that aggregate food price analysis obscures important heterogeneity. Each commodity requires its own model specification.

6.
Limitation
Limitación
Method Limitation
Método Limitación
The multiple regression assumes a linear, additive relationship between determinants and rice prices. Interaction effects (e.g., the exchange rate effect may be stronger during non-harvest months when imports are more critical) are not tested. Additionally, the 48-month sample size limits the number of variables and lags that can be reliably included without overfitting. Government price support programs (garantia de compra) introduce price floors that may bias coefficient estimates.
7.
Policy Implication
Implicaciones de Política

The finding that exchange rate explains 38% of rice price variance — more than harvest seasonality — has direct implications for Colombia's rice price stabilization policy. Currency hedging instruments for rice importers and processors could reduce price volatility more effectively than current storage-based interventions that target seasonal fluctuations.

For FEDEARROZ and the Ministry of Agriculture, the quantified seasonal amplitude (+/-12%) provides empirical grounding for harvest timing recommendations and storage incentive calibration. Price forecasting models should weight exchange rate movements more heavily than production calendar factors.
