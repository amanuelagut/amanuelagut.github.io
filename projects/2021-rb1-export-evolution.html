Colombian Agricultural Export Evolution: Key Trading Partners 2015–2021

Angie Manuela Gutierrez-Oviedo · Fundación Universitaria del Área Andina · 2021
UN Comtrade HS 01-24 Window Functions Partner Share CAGR Export Diversification
Abstract
Resumen
This brief traces the evolution of Colombian agricultural exports to key trading partners over 2015–2021 using SQL queries on UN Comtrade bilateral trade data. The analysis computes annual export values, partner shares, and compound annual growth rates (CAGR) for Colombia's top 15 agricultural export destinations. The findings reveal a gradual diversification: the United States' share declined from 38.2% to 34.8% (−3.4 pp), while Asian markets (Japan + South Korea) gained from 5.5% to 8.7% (+3.2 pp). Total agricultural exports grew from USD 7.12 billion (2015) to USD 8.94 billion (2021), a cumulative increase of 25.6% over the period (CAGR = 3.9%).
Keywords: agricultural exports, trade partners, export diversification, Colombia, UN Comtrade, SQL analysis, window functions
1.
Research Question
Pregunta de Investigación

How did Colombian agricultural exports to key trading partners evolve between 2015 and 2021, and which partners gained or lost market share?

H₀ (Null Hypothesis): Colombian agricultural export shares across trading partners remained stable between 2015 and 2021, with no significant shift in partner concentration (Herfindahl-Hirschman Index change < 100 points).

H₁ (Alternative Hypothesis): Colombian agricultural export shares shifted significantly over the period, with emerging Asian markets gaining share from traditional North American and European partners, reducing overall export concentration.

Colombia's agricultural export profile is historically concentrated in coffee (HS 0901) and cut flowers (HS 0603) directed to the US and EU. Understanding whether geographic diversification is occurring — beyond product diversification — is relevant for assessing trade vulnerability and the effectiveness of export promotion policies under the Colombia-Korea FTA (2016) and Pacific Alliance initiatives.

2.
Data & Sources
Datos y Fuentes

Bilateral export data is sourced from UN Comtrade, the official repository for international trade statistics maintained by the United Nations Statistics Division. Agricultural products are defined as HS chapters 01–24.

The dataset contains 7 years × ~180 partner countries × ~1,200 HS 6-digit codes, totaling approximately 285,000 bilateral trade records. The top 15 partners by cumulative 2015–2021 value are selected for share analysis, accounting for 89.3% of total agricultural exports.

3.
Methodology
Metodología

The analysis uses progressively complex SQL queries to extract and compute partner-level export dynamics.

Step 1 — Aggregate exports by partner and year. SELECT partner, year, SUM(trade_value) FROM comtrade WHERE reporter='Colombia' AND flow='Export' AND hs_chapter BETWEEN 1 AND 24 GROUP BY partner, year computes annual export totals for each destination.

Step 2 — Partner share via window function. SUM(value) OVER (PARTITION BY year) computes annual totals, enabling share calculation as partner_value / year_total × 100 without subqueries.

Step 3 — CAGR computation. Compound Annual Growth Rate = ((V₂₀₂₁ / V₂₀₁₅)^(1/6) − 1) × 100, computed for each partner with non-zero trade in both endpoint years.

Step 4 — Share change ranking. Partners ranked by absolute change in share (2021 share − 2015 share) to identify gainers and losers.

4.
Key Finding
Hallazgo Principal

The partner evolution analysis reveals gradual but measurable geographic diversification in Colombia's agricultural export portfolio.

Key Finding
Hallazgo Principal
United States share declined from 38.2% to 34.8% (−3.4 pp) despite absolute value growth from USD 2.72B to USD 3.11B (CAGR +2.3%). The US remains the largest partner but its dominance is slowly eroding.

European Union share declined from 24.5% to 22.1% (−2.4 pp), with Netherlands remaining the primary EU gateway for Colombian flowers and coffee.

Japan + South Korea combined grew from 5.5% to 8.7% (+3.2 pp), the largest regional gain. Japan's CAGR of +12.4% reflects growing demand for Colombian specialty coffee; South Korea's +14.1% CAGR follows the 2016 FTA implementation.

Intra-Andean trade (Ecuador + Peru) grew from 6.6% to 8.2% (+1.6 pp), driven by processed food categories under CAN preferential tariffs.

Total agricultural exports: USD 7.12B (2015) → USD 8.94B (2021), a 25.6% cumulative increase (CAGR = 3.9%).

The diversification trend reduces Colombia's dependence on any single trading partner. The Herfindahl-Hirschman Index (HHI) for partner concentration declined from 2,340 (2015) to 2,180 (2021), a 160-point improvement indicating moderately less concentrated exports.

5.
What I Learned
Lo que Aprendí

SQL window functions for share computation (SUM() OVER (PARTITION BY year)) introduced partition-based analysis — computing metrics within groups without collapsing the data. This pattern directly informs the groupby().transform() operations used in Python pandas in 2024.

The experience of interpreting trade share changes — distinguishing between absolute growth and relative share shifts — built analytical intuition for proportional reasoning that applies across all economic analysis. A partner can grow in absolute terms while losing share if others grow faster.

Structuring CAGR calculations in SQL using the POWER() function taught compound growth computation at the database level, eliminating the need for spreadsheet post-processing.

6.
Limitation
Limitación
Scope Limitation
Alcance de la Limitación
The analysis does not disaggregate by product within each partner relationship. A partner gaining share might be driven entirely by one product (e.g., specialty coffee to Japan) rather than broad-based diversification across the agricultural portfolio. The 2021 Capstone addresses this limitation with product × partner cross-tabulations. Additionally, value-based shares can be affected by price changes; volume-based analysis would provide complementary insights.
7.
Policy Implication
Implicaciones de Política

The gradual diversification of Colombia's agricultural export destinations toward Asian markets suggests that trade promotion efforts and free trade agreements (Colombia-Korea FTA, 2016) are beginning to show measurable effects. However, the slow pace of change — with the US still commanding over one-third of exports — indicates continued vulnerability to demand shocks in traditional markets.

For ProColombia and the Ministry of Commerce, the finding supports continued investment in Asian market access: the +14.1% CAGR in South Korea demonstrates FTA utilization, while the +12.4% CAGR in Japan (without an FTA) suggests untapped potential. Policy makers should consider accelerating CPTPP accession negotiations to expand preferential access in high-growth Asian markets.
