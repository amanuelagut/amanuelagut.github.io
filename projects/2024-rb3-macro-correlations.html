Research Brief 3 2024 JEL: E44, F31, Q11 · 2015 – 2024 · Python (seaborn, statsmodels)

Macroeconomic Indicators and Agricultural Price Correlations in Colombia 2015–2024

Angie Manuela Gutierrez-Oviedo · Fundación Universitaria del Área Andina · 2024
FRED Banco de la República seaborn statsmodels Correlation Matrix Macro-Agri Linkages
Abstract
Resumen
This brief systematically maps the correlations between Colombian macroeconomic indicators — interest rates, inflation, exchange rate — and agricultural commodity prices between 2015 and 2024. Using Python's seaborn for heatmap visualization and statsmodels for significance testing, the analysis produces a comprehensive correlation matrix identifying the USD/COP exchange rate as the dominant price transmission channel (r = 0.847, p < 0.001). The Brent oil-fertilizer cost correlation (r = 0.934) represents the strongest linkage, while interest rates show weaker, indirect effects. All correlations weakened during the 2020–2021 pandemic period and strengthened during 2022–2023 normalization.
Keywords: correlation matrix, macroeconomic indicators, agricultural prices, exchange rate, inflation, seaborn heatmap, Colombia
1.
Research Question
Pregunta de Investigación

Which macroeconomic indicators — interest rates, inflation, exchange rate — show the strongest correlations with agricultural prices in Colombia between 2015 and 2024?

H₀ (Null Hypothesis): No single macroeconomic indicator shows dominant correlation with agricultural prices; all macro-price correlations are approximately equal in magnitude (|r| difference < 0.20 across indicators).

H₁ (Alternative Hypothesis): The exchange rate dominates other macroeconomic indicators as the primary price transmission channel, showing significantly stronger correlation (|r| difference > 0.20) with agricultural prices than interest rates or domestic inflation.

The 2020 and 2022 portfolios tested individual macro-price relationships (exchange rate, oil prices). This brief takes a systematic approach: mapping all pairwise correlations simultaneously to identify the strongest linkages and avoid the cherry-picking risk of testing hypotheses one at a time.

3.
Methodology
Metodología

The analysis produces a comprehensive correlation matrix with significance testing and time-varying stability analysis.

Step 1 — Seaborn heatmap correlation matrix. All pairwise Pearson correlations computed with df.corr() and visualized as a seaborn heatmap using sns.heatmap(corr_matrix, annot=True, cmap='RdYlGn') with color-coded significance levels (p < 0.05 highlighted).

Step 2 — Significance testing. For each of the 45 unique pairs, the null hypothesis of zero correlation is tested using scipy.stats.pearsonr(), with Bonferroni correction for multiple comparisons (α = 0.05/45 = 0.0011).

Step 3 — Rolling correlation stability. 5-year rolling correlations computed using df.rolling(60).corr() for the strongest relationships to test whether they are stable over time or structurally variable.

4.
Key Finding
Hallazgo Principal

The systematic correlation mapping reveals that exchange rate effects dominate the macro-agricultural price relationship in Colombia, confirming H₁.

Key Finding
Hallazgo Principal
USD/COP ↔ Brent oil: r = 0.847 (p < 0.001) — the exchange rate-oil price nexus is the primary transmission channel, as oil prices drive both import costs and currency pressure.

Inflation ↔ Food CPI: r = 0.892 (p < 0.001) — unsurprising as food is a major CPI component, but confirms tight short-term pass-through from agricultural to consumer prices.

Brent oil ↔ Fertilizer cost: r = 0.934 (p < 0.001) — the strongest correlation in the matrix, reflecting the energy-intensive nature of fertilizer production.

Interest rate ↔ USD/COP: r = 0.651 (p < 0.001) — moderate correlation suggests indirect transmission from monetary policy to agricultural prices via the exchange rate channel.

GDP growth ↔ Imports: r = −0.498 (p < 0.001) — the only significant negative correlation, reflecting import compression during economic contractions.

H₁ confirmed: Exchange rate correlations exceed other macro indicators by >0.20, establishing it as the dominant price transmission channel.

The exchange rate's dominance as a macro-agricultural price link reinforces its centrality in the 2025 working paper's analytical framework. The rolling correlation analysis reveals that all relationships weakened during 2020–2021 (pandemic disruption) and strengthened during 2022–2023 (normalization), confirming the 2022 structural break finding.

5.
What I Learned
Lo que Aprendí

Seaborn heatmaps for correlation matrices replaced the manual Excel correlation tables of 2022 with a visualization that instantly communicates the full pattern of relationships — making it possible to identify unexpected correlations that targeted hypothesis testing would miss.

Bonferroni correction for multiple comparisons introduced the critical concept that testing many hypotheses simultaneously inflates false positive rates. With 45 pairwise tests, the uncorrected 5% significance level would expect 2.25 false positives by chance; Bonferroni correction (α = 0.0011) ensures family-wise error control.

Rolling correlation analysis formally captures the time-varying nature of economic relationships — extending the static correlation comparisons of 2022 into a continuous dynamic measure that reveals when relationships break down and recover.

6.
Limitation
Limitación
Method Limitation
Limitación del Método
Correlation does not imply causation, and contemporaneous correlations cannot distinguish between leading, lagging, and spurious relationships. The Brent-fertilizer correlation (r = 0.934) could reflect common cause (global supply chain) rather than direct transmission. The 2025 working paper addresses this with Granger causality tests and instrumental variable approaches that can identify directional causality.
7.
Policy Implication
Implicaciones de Política

The dominance of the exchange rate channel in macro-agricultural price transmission has direct policy implications for Colombia's agricultural sector management. Exchange rate stabilization policies — or hedging instruments for farmers — may be more effective at controlling agricultural price volatility than domestic monetary policy interventions.

The strong Brent-fertilizer correlation (r = 0.934) suggests that energy price shocks transmit rapidly to agricultural input costs. For policy makers, this implies that energy subsidy policies have indirect agricultural price stability effects, and that fertilizer stockpiling or strategic reserves could buffer against oil price volatility. The finding supports integrated energy-agricultural policy frameworks rather than siloed sectoral approaches.
