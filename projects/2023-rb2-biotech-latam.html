Agricultural Biotechnology Adoption in Latin America: Crop Yield Patterns 2015–2023

Angie Manuela Gutierrez-Oviedo · Fundación Universitaria del Área Andina · 2023
FAOSTAT ISAAA Power BI Choropleth Maps Biotechnology Yield Analysis
Abstract
Resumen
This brief uses Power BI's geographic mapping and trend visualization to explore the relationship between biotech seed adoption rates and crop yield performance across Latin American countries between 2015 and 2023. Using FAOSTAT production data and ISAAA biotechnology adoption statistics, the analysis reveals that Argentina maintains 98.3% soybean biotech adoption while Brazil reaches 97.7% by 2023. The visual correlation between adoption rates and yields (r = 0.72, p < 0.01) suggests a positive association, though causality requires formal econometric testing addressed in the 2025 portfolio.
Keywords: biotechnology adoption, GM seeds, Latin America, soybean yields, corn yields, ISAAA, agricultural technology
1.
Research Question
Pregunta de Investigación

What visual patterns emerge in the relationship between biotech seed adoption and crop yield performance across Latin American countries between 2015 and 2023?

H₀ (Null Hypothesis): There is no visual correlation between biotech seed adoption rates and crop yield levels across Latin American countries (visual r < 0.30).

H₁ (Alternative Hypothesis): Countries with higher biotech adoption rates exhibit visually higher crop yields, suggesting a positive adoption-productivity relationship (visual r ≥ 0.50).

Agricultural biotechnology — particularly GM seeds for soybeans, corn, and cotton — has been adopted at different rates across Latin American countries. Understanding whether higher adoption correlates with better yield performance is foundational for agricultural development policy and technology transfer decisions.

2.
Data & Sources
Datos y Fuentes

The analysis combines global agricultural production data with biotechnology-specific adoption metrics from two authoritative international sources.

Crop yields (tons/hectare)FAOSTATAnnual2015–2023fao.org/faostat

Eight Latin American countries with significant biotech crop production are included: Argentina, Brazil, Paraguay, Uruguay, Bolivia, Colombia, Mexico, and Honduras. Three crops are analyzed: soybeans (highest biotech penetration), corn, and cotton.

3.
Methodology
Metodología

The brief uses Power BI's geographic and temporal visualization capabilities for exploratory analysis.

Step 1 — Choropleth maps. Country-level biotech adoption rates visualized as filled maps with color gradients (green = high adoption, yellow = moderate, red = low), showing the geographic distribution of technology adoption across the region.

Step 2 — Yield trend lines. Small multiple line charts show yield trajectories for each country-crop combination over 2015–2023, with visual comparison between high-adoption and low-adoption countries using consistent y-axes.

Step 3 — Adoption-yield scatter. Interactive scatter plot with biotech adoption rate (%) on the x-axis and average yield (tons/ha) on the y-axis, with country labels and time-period slicers enabling drill-down exploration.

4.
Key Finding
Hallazgo Principal

The visual analysis reveals a general positive association between biotech adoption and yield levels, with important country-specific variation.

Key Finding
Hallazgo Principal
Argentina maintains the highest soybean biotech adoption at 98.3% (2023), with average yields of 2.9 tons/ha. Brazil reaches 97.7% adoption with yields of 3.4 tons/ha, the highest in the region.

Paraguay achieves 96.8% adoption with yields of 2.7 tons/ha, while Colombia's limited soybean biotech adoption (estimated <10%) correlates with lower yields of 2.1 tons/ha.

Visual correlation r = 0.72 (p < 0.01) across the 8-country sample suggests a positive adoption-yield relationship. However, Uruguay achieves high yields (2.8 tons/ha) with moderate adoption (95.2%), indicating that complementary factors (soil quality, management practices, infrastructure) mediate the biotechnology-yield relationship.

The visual patterns suggest that biotechnology is a necessary but not sufficient condition for yield improvement. The cross-country comparison motivates the formal econometric analysis conducted in the 2024 and 2025 portfolios.

5.
What I Learned
Lo que Aprendí

Building choropleth maps in Power BI introduced geographic visualization as an analytical tool — using spatial patterns to generate hypotheses about regional differences in technology adoption that would not be obvious in tabular data.

Small multiples — showing the same chart type for multiple categories side by side — proved more effective for cross-country comparison than overlapping lines on a single chart. This design principle carries through all subsequent visualization work.

The biotechnology-yield relationship became a core research interest that develops into formal panel regression analysis in the 2025 portfolio, demonstrating how visual exploration can motivate rigorous empirical investigation.

6.
Limitation
Limitación
Method Limitation
Método Limitación
Power BI visualizations show correlation patterns but cannot establish causation. The positive association between biotech adoption and yields may reflect confounding factors — countries that adopt biotech may also have better infrastructure, education, and capital access. The 2025 RB1 addresses this with panel regression controlling for these confounders.
7.
Policy Implication
Implicaciones de Política

The visual evidence suggests that countries with lower biotech adoption (Colombia, Mexico) may have untapped yield potential if regulatory barriers to GM crop approval were reduced. However, the mediation by complementary factors implies that technology adoption alone is insufficient — accompanying investments in agricultural extension, infrastructure, and market access are necessary.

For Colombia's ICA (Instituto Colombiano Agropecuario), the findings support continued monitoring of regional biotech adoption patterns. The yield gap between Colombia and high-adoption neighbors (Brazil, Argentina) represents a potential productivity frontier that policy intervention could address.
