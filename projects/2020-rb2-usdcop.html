USD/COP Exchange Rate and Grain Import Costs in Colombia 2018–2020

Angie Manuela Gutierrez-Oviedo · Fundación Universitaria del Área Andina · 2020
FRED Data COLCCUSMA02STM DCOILBRENTEU Exchange Rate Brent Crude Oil Correlation Analysis Base-100 Index COVID-19
Abstract
Resumen
This brief examines the relationship between the USD/COP exchange rate and Brent crude oil prices during 2018–2020, with emphasis on the COVID-19 shock period. Using monthly data from FRED (Federal Reserve Economic Data), the analysis documents a 43.7% depreciation of the Colombian peso from its April 2018 low (COP 2,765/USD) to its April 2020 peak (COP 3,974/USD), coinciding with a 76.0% collapse in Brent crude prices (from $81.42/bbl in October 2018 to $19.53/bbl in April 2020). The strong negative correlation (r = −0.82) between these variables reflects Colombia's oil-dependent economy: when global oil demand collapsed, USD inflows from petroleum exports declined, triggering peso depreciation. For grain importers, this created a dual shock — while transport costs fell with oil prices, imported agricultural inputs became significantly more expensive in peso terms.
1.
Research Question
Pregunta de Investigación

How did the USD/COP exchange rate respond to the global oil price collapse during COVID-19, and what were the implications for grain import costs in Colombia?

Colombia is both a net oil exporter and a net grain importer. The country imports approximately 80% of its corn and 95% of its wheat consumption. Understanding the transmission mechanism from global oil prices to the exchange rate — and from the exchange rate to import costs — is essential for modeling agricultural price dynamics in an open, commodity-dependent economy.

2.
Data & Sources
Datos y Fuentes

All macroeconomic data is sourced from FRED (Federal Reserve Economic Data), the official data repository of the Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis. FRED provides standardized, quality-controlled time series widely used in academic research and central bank analyses.

The dataset spans 36 months, capturing the pre-pandemic baseline (2018–2019), the acute shock (March–April 2020), and the partial recovery (May–December 2020). Daily Brent prices were aggregated to monthly averages to match the USD/COP frequency.

3.
Methodology
Metodología

The analysis applies five analytical steps to quantify the exchange rate shock and its correlation with oil prices.

Step 1 — Descriptive statistics. Mean, standard deviation, minimum, maximum, range, and coefficient of variation (CV) computed for each variable across the full 36-month period and for annual subperiods (2018, 2019, 2020).

Step 2 — Base-100 price index. Both series are re-expressed as (P_t / P_Jan2018) × 100, enabling direct visual comparison of relative movements on a common scale.

Step 3 — Month-over-month change (MoM%). Computed as ((P_t − P_t−1) / P_t−1) × 100 to identify the timing and magnitude of the sharpest movements.

Step 4 — Pearson correlation. Pairwise correlation coefficient (r) between USD/COP and Brent across all 36 months, testing the hypothesis that peso depreciation tracks oil price declines.

Step 5 — Period comparison. Pre-COVID (Mar–Dec 2019) vs. COVID (Mar–Dec 2020) comparison of mean exchange rate and implied import cost increase.

4.
Key Finding
Hallazgo Principal

The analysis documents a synchronized shock: as Brent crude collapsed, the Colombian peso depreciated sharply, creating a direct cost transmission channel to grain imports.

Key Finding
Hallazgo Principal
USD/COP depreciated 43.7% from its April 2018 low of COP 2,765.23/USD to its April 2020 peak of COP 3,973.60/USD. The sharpest single-month movement was +14.4% MoM in March 2020 (COP 3,415 → COP 3,908), coinciding with the global risk-off event triggered by COVID-19.

Brent crude collapsed 76.0% from its October 2018 peak of $81.42/barrel to its April 2020 trough of $19.53/barrel — the lowest level since 2002. The sharpest declines were −42.7% MoM in March 2020 ($55.43 → $31.78) and −38.5% MoM in April 2020 ($31.78 → $19.53).

Correlation: r = −0.82 across the 36-month series, confirming a strong inverse relationship. When oil prices fell, the peso depreciated; when oil recovered (May–December 2020), the peso partially strengthened.

Import cost impact: A $1,000 USD grain shipment that cost COP 3.31 million in March–December 2019 would cost COP 3.76 million in the same period of 2020 — a +13.6% increase in peso terms, purely from exchange rate movement.

The base-100 index (January 2018 = 100) reveals the magnitude of divergence: by April 2020, USD/COP reached index 138.6 while Brent collapsed to index 28.3 — a 110-point spread that captures the "twin shock" facing Colombian importers.

5.
What I Learned
Lo que Aprendí

This project introduced the concept of macro-agricultural linkages — the transmission channels through which global commodity prices (oil) affect domestic agricultural costs through intermediate variables (exchange rate). This framing reappears in the 2024 Capstone's multi-factor regression.

FRED as a data source became a core resource for subsequent projects. The standardized series IDs (COLCCUSMA02STM, DCOILBRENTEU) enable reproducible API calls, and the CSV export format integrates directly with Excel and Python workflows.

The base-100 index technique from RB1 was extended here to compare two series with different units (COP/USD vs. USD/barrel) on a common scale — a visualization standard used by Banco de la República and DANE in their official publications.

6.
Limitation
Limitación
Scope Limitation
Alcance Limitación
The analysis treats the exchange rate as an exogenous variable, without modeling feedback effects from domestic agricultural prices. The correlation is contemporaneous — no lag structure is tested. A more complete model would include: (1) import volume data to quantify actual cost pass-through, (2) freight rate indices to separate currency effects from logistics costs, and (3) a VAR or Granger causality test to establish temporal precedence. Additionally, the analysis does not account for exchange rate hedging instruments used by large importers.
7.
Deliverable
Entregable

The deliverable is an Excel workbook (.xlsx) with four worksheets: Raw_Data (FRED USD/COP and Brent monthly series, 36 observations each), Descriptive_Stats (annual and full-period statistics, MoM% series, period comparisons), Correlation (Pearson matrix with scatter plot data), and Charts_Data (base-100 index values for both series).
