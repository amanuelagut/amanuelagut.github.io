Fríjol Price Volatility in Colombia: First 6 Months of COVID

Angie Manuela Gutierrez-Oviedo · Fundación Universitaria del Área Andina · 2020
SIPSA/DANE Corabastos · Bogotá Descriptive Statistics Coefficient of Variation Price Indexing T-Test · OLS FRED Data COVID-19
Abstract
Resumen
This brief examines whether COVID-19 disruptions significantly increased the price volatility of wholesale fríjol (common bean) in Colombia's main agricultural market during March–August 2020, compared to the same period in 2019. Using SIPSA-DANE wholesale price data from Corabastos (Bogotá) alongside USD/COP exchange rate and Brent crude oil series from FRED, the analysis documents the magnitude and timing of the price shock through descriptive statistics, Welch's t-test, base-100 price indexing, Pearson correlation, and OLS regression. The Coefficient of Variation of fríjol prices nearly doubled (+88.4%) between the two periods, with the sharpest single-month surge of +28.3% occurring in April 2020 — concurrent with the national lockdown declaration and a 125.7-index reading for the USD/COP rate. Fríjol is Colombia's primary vegetable protein source, making it a relevant case study for food price risk under supply chain stress.
1.
Research Question
Pregunta de Investigación

Did COVID-19 disruptions increase wholesale fríjol price volatility in Colombia's main market (Corabastos, Bogotá) during March–August 2020, compared to the same period in 2019? And if so, by how much, and from which point in the pandemic timeline did divergence begin?

The question is motivated by direct operational observation: during the first months of the pandemic, supply chain disruptions in Colombian food distribution networks were significant, with mobility restrictions affecting both transport and market access. Fríjol — as a storable staple with concentrated wholesale distribution — provides a clean case for measuring price transmission of a supply shock.

2.
Data & Sources
Datos y Fuentes

All data for this project is drawn from SIPSA (Sistema de Información de Precios del Sector Agropecuario), the official agricultural price information system of Colombia's national statistics agency DANE. SIPSA publishes weekly wholesale prices by product, market, and city — the same source cited in Banco de la República analyses of food inflation.

3.
Methodology
Metodología

The reconstructed analysis uses Python (pandas, scipy, statsmodels) on the same SIPSA-DANE source data, extended with two macroeconomic variables from FRED to contextualize the price shock. Seven analytical steps are applied sequentially.

Step 1 — Month-over-month change (MoM%). Computed as ((P_t − P_t−1) / P_t−1) × 100 for each of the 20 months. Identifies the speed and direction of price movement, separating trend from volatility.

Step 2 — Descriptive statistics by period. Two comparison blocks (Mar–Aug 2019 vs. Mar–Aug 2020), each reporting mean, standard deviation, min, max, range, and CV. Establishes the baseline before formal testing.

Step 3 — Coefficient of Variation (CV). CV = (SD / mean) × 100. The central volatility metric: normalizes dispersion relative to the price level, enabling cross-period comparison even when the mean itself changes.

Step 4 — Welch's t-test. Two-sample t-test with unequal variances compares the mean fríjol price between the two six-month periods. Reports t-statistic, approximate degrees of freedom (Welch–Satterthwaite), two-tailed p-value, and Cohen's d for practical effect size.

Step 5 — Base-100 price index. All three series (fríjol, USD/COP, Brent) are re-expressed as (P_t / P_Jan2019) × 100. The resulting chart reveals the relative magnitude of each shock on a common scale.

Step 6 — Pearson correlation matrix. Pairwise correlations across the full 20-month series (n = 20) for fríjol, USD/COP, and Brent. Concurrent monthly data, no lag adjustment.

Step 7 — OLS regression. Fríjolt = β₀ + β₁·USD/COPt + β₂·Brentt + ε. Reports coefficients, standard errors, t-statistics, R², and adjusted R². The high collinearity between USD/COP and Brent (r = −0.90) limits the interpretability of individual coefficients — a limitation discussed in Section 6.

4.
Key Finding
Hallazgo Principal

The analysis documents a clear and measurable volatility increase in wholesale fríjol prices during the first six months of COVID-19 restrictions, over and above the mean price shift.

Key Finding
Hallazgo Principal
The Coefficient of Variation of wholesale fríjol prices at Corabastos nearly doubled between the two six-month periods: CV = 6.02% (Mar–Aug 2019) → 11.34% (Mar–Aug 2020), an increase of +88.4%. The mean price rose from COP 5,892/kg to COP 6,531/kg (+10.8%), but the volatility increase was eight times larger in relative terms — indicating genuine instability rather than a simple level shift. The sharpest monthly surge was +28.3% MoM in April 2020, the largest in the 20-month series, coinciding with the national lockdown declared on March 20, 2020. Prices peaked at COP 7,317/kg in May 2020 (index: 125.9, base Jan 2019 = 100), before partially recovering to COP 5,978/kg by August 2020. Welch's t-test: t = 1.91, df = 7.18, p = 0.097 — not significant at α = 0.05 given n = 6 per period, but Cohen's d = 1.10 indicates a large practical effect size. Contextually, Brent crude collapsed to 53.4% of its January 2019 value by March 2020, while the USD/COP rate peaked at index 125.7 in April 2020.

The finding confirms that volatility — not just the price level — increased during the pandemic period. The OLS regression (R² = 0.36, R²adj = 0.29) is constrained by high collinearity between USD/COP and Brent (r = −0.90), but the base-100 index clearly shows that fríjol prices moved in the same direction as the currency depreciation and opposite to the oil crash — a pattern consistent with supply chain disruption rather than cost-push inflation alone.

5.
What I Learned
Lo que Aprendí

This project introduced the coefficient of variation as the first normalizing statistic I applied — the insight that comparing raw standard deviations across periods with different mean prices is methodologically flawed, and that CV resolves this by expressing dispersion relative to the mean. This principle reappears in the 2022 Capstone and the 2025 RB2 (formal GARCH in Python).

Base-100 indexing is the standard language of economic reporting — IPC, terms of trade, equity indices all use this convention. Building it manually from a fixed reference point, with absolute cell references in Google Sheets, makes the underlying logic explicit.

The discipline of separating raw data from analysis (Raw_Data, Analysis, and Chart as three separate sheets) establishes a reproducibility habit that scales directly to Jupyter Notebooks in 2024–2025.

6.
Limitation
Limitación
Scope Limitation
Alcance Limitación
The analysis covers a single market (Corabastos, Bogotá) and aggregated variety data. It does not capture regional price heterogeneity across Colombia's distributed wholesale markets, nor does it control for variety-specific supply factors. The OLS regression is further limited by severe multicollinearity between the two regressors (USD/COP and Brent, r = −0.90 over the 20-month window), which inflates coefficient standard errors and prevents reliable isolation of each variable's independent effect. Additionally, the t-test comparison has n = 6 per period, limiting statistical power despite a large Cohen's d. A multi-market, multi-variety extension — the kind made feasible by SQL queries in 2021 — and a lagged-variable or VAR specification would produce a more structurally robust finding.
7.
Deliverable
Entregable

The deliverable is an Excel workbook (.xlsx) with five worksheets: Raw_Data (SIPSA-DANE fríjol prices, FRED USD/COP, FRED Brent monthly averages), Descriptive_Stats (period comparison table, Welch's t-test, window analysis, MoM% series), Correlation (Pearson matrix with source note), Regression (OLS coefficients, fitted values, residuals), and Charts_Data (base-100 index values and box plot inputs).
